Module 1 · Information systems

From transaction records to managed performance.

One connected journey: AIS turns business events into accounting information; ERP connects the enterprise; EPM turns actuals into plans, forecasts and performance decisions.

1 / 6
Question
Are AIS, ERP and EPM three names for the same system?

They often appear in the same finance conversation—but they solve different problems.

Teaching objective
Give learners one clean mental model before introducing modules, vendors or technical detail.
AIS · Accounting Information System

AIS turns transactions into accounting information.

TPS

Capture

Processes routine transactions such as sales, purchases, payroll and receipts.

GL / FRS

Report

Posts and summarizes accounting entries into the general ledger and financial statements.

MRS

Manage

Produces budgets, operating reports, CVP analysis and other internal information.

AIS is not only accounting software. It is the process by which accounting data becomes usable information.
ERP · Enterprise Resource Planning

ERP connects business processes on shared data.

Sales
Purchasing
Finance & GL
Inventory
Production / HR

Instead of separate systems repeatedly sending files to Finance, ERP integrates workflows and uses a common data model.

ERP does not create one truth by magic. It creates the possibility of one truth—if processes, definitions and master data are governed.
EPM · Enterprise Performance Management

EPM turns actual performance into forward-looking management.

ERP runs the business. EPM helps management steer it.

Module 3 · Data Governance

Trust is designed before data is analyzed.

A finance-led journey through governance, lifecycle, COSO controls, master data, security and decision trust.

1 / 7
Opening question
Who owns a number after it leaves the system that created it?

Data Governance is not an IT clean-up activity. It defines who may create, change, use, share and protect data—and under what rules.

Usability

Can people use it?

Users know the meaning, location and proper use of data.

Availability

Is it there?

Data is accessible when the decision or process needs it.

Integrity

Can it be trusted?

Data remains accurate, complete and protected from manipulation.

Security

Who may touch it?

Access is limited and monitored according to legitimate need.

Why governance matters

Better decisions require more than better dashboards.

Governance creates

  • Trust in data used for decisions
  • Regulatory and policy compliance
  • Fewer errors, inconsistencies and misuse
  • Efficient data sharing and analytics

Finance lens

A report can be mathematically correct and still be operationally misleading if definitions, ownership, access or timing are uncontrolled.

The right data must reach the right decision-maker, for the right purpose, at the right time.

Opening question
When does buying new technology fail to create transformation?

Northstar Finance bought workflow tools, yet month-end still takes 12 days. The team automated tasks but kept duplicate approvals, inconsistent data and spreadsheet handoffs.

Digitizing a broken process produces a faster broken process.

Finance role: protect business requirements, accounting logic, controls, data definitions and benefits realization.
Business Process Analysis

Remove waste before automating work.

Current close

  • 47 spreadsheet handoffs
  • Duplicated approvals
  • Manual account matching
  • Late issue discovery
→

Future close

  • One controlled workflow
  • Exception-based review
  • Standard reconciliation rules
  • Visible ownership and status
Ask first: eliminate, simplify, standardize—then automate.
Automation ladder

Not every problem needs AI.

Workflow

Route work, approvals and exceptions using defined rules.

RPA

Repeat stable, high-volume, rules-based actions across systems.

AI

Interpret patterns, unstructured data, anomalies or uncertain outcomes.

Human judgment

Resolve ambiguity, ethics, material exceptions and accountability.

Selection test: How stable are the rules? How structured is the data? How costly is an error?
RPA and AI in Finance

Automation is strongest when the task matches the tool.

RPA

Execute defined actions

  • Account reconciliations
  • Posting invoices
  • Updating daily reports
  • Sending routine notifications

Weak fit: variable workflow, human judgment, changing rules or unstructured inputs.

AI

Interpret and recommend

  • Anomaly and fraud detection
  • Document classification
  • Forecasting and planning
  • Contract review and Q&A

Control need: reliable data, validation, explainability and human oversight.

Cloud Computing

Cloud changes access, scale and the cost structure.

IaaS

Rent infrastructure

Servers, storage and networking. More control, more technical responsibility.

PaaS

Rent a platform

Managed environment for teams to develop and deploy applications.

SaaS

Subscribe to software

Fast access, simpler maintenance, scalable users and CAPEX-to-OPEX shift.

Value

Speed, standardization, scalability and lower infrastructure burden.

Risk

Security, cost overruns, service dependency and difficult switching.

Finance question

Does the commercial model preserve control, resilience and total-cost visibility?

Blockchain and Smart Contracts

Use distributed trust only when the business problem needs it.

Shared record

Multiple parties need a verifiable, timestamped and auditable history.

Limited trust

No single participant should control or rewrite the complete record.

Programmable action

A smart contract can execute predetermined rules when conditions are met.

Do not use blockchain merely because immutability sounds attractive. A governed centralized database is often simpler and cheaper.

Module 5 · Data Analytics

Move from reporting the past to improving the decision.

Business intelligence, data progression, analytics levels, data mining, models, correlation, regression and visualization—translated into Finance decisions.

1 / 10
Opening question
When does a dashboard become analytics?

A dashboard can describe performance beautifully and still leave management unsure why it happened or what to do next.

Business Intelligence delivers information. Analytics deepens explanation, prediction and action.
Data progression

Value increases as context and judgment are added.

01

Data

Raw records and observations.

02

Information

Organized, aggregated and contextualized.

03

Insight

Patterns interpreted using business knowledge.

04

Decision

A choice under objectives and constraints.

05

Action

Execution, learning and measurable outcomes.

Finance Lens: the value of analytics is not the model. It is the quality of the decision and action it enables.
Analytics workflow

Start with a decision—not a dataset.

Define question
Prepare data
Explore patterns
Analyze & test
Communicate & act
A precise answer to the wrong business question is still a poor analysis.
Four levels of analytics

The management question determines the analytics level.

Descriptive

What happened?

Trends, relationships, totals and visual summaries.

Diagnostic

Why did it happen?

Drill-down, data mining, regression and root-cause investigation.

Predictive

What may happen?

Forecasts, probabilities, time series and machine learning.

Prescriptive

What should we do?

Optimization, sensitivity, scenarios and recommended actions.

Data Mining

Patterns are hypotheses—not automatic truth.

What data mining seeks

  • Associations between events
  • Sequences where one event follows another
  • Correlations and recurring patterns
  • Clusters or classifications

What can go wrong

  • Biased or poor-quality data
  • Complex matching across sources
  • Statistical significance without practical value
  • Privacy, regulation and excessive outputs
Data mining must be tempered with domain knowledge and common sense.
Analytic model toolkit

Choose a model by the decision structure.

Clustering

Discover natural groups when categories are not predetermined.

Classification

Assign observations to predefined groups, such as default or no default.

Regression

Estimate relationships between a response and explanatory variables.

Time series

Use trend, seasonal, cyclical and random patterns over time.

Sensitivity

Test how changes in assumptions affect an outcome.

Simulation

Run many possible scenarios to understand the distribution of outcomes.

Teacher mode

Landmarks, not a script.

The lecturer preserves personal style. These notes standardize the intended cognitive movement and keep the session easy to recover if timing drifts.

Non-negotiable outcome

  • Break the belief that technology belongs only to IT.
  • Make the learner see the chain from business event to decision.
  • End with increased confidence—not technical intimidation.

Suggested opening

  • Ask the big question and allow disagreement.
  • Do not explain the answer immediately.
  • Keep the opening under five minutes.

Aha to protect

  • Reports are downstream outputs.
  • Small data-quality issues compound as information becomes insight and decision.
  • Finance can participate without coding.

Performance space

  • Replace the three-revenue example with a real company story.
  • Use humor, audience questions or discussion freely.
  • Shorten or extend simulation debrief according to the room.

MVP operating rule

  • Use only tools that recur during a normal class.
  • Handle unusual classroom situations through Cue and Discussion—not separate modes.
  • Keep the lecturer's attention on the room, not the toolbar.

Common failure

  • Turning the opening into a lecture about AI trends.
  • Explaining every technology term.
  • Making the learner feel behind or inadequate.

Systems journey

  • Protect the clean distinction: AIS transforms transactions into accounting information; ERP integrates enterprise processes; EPM manages plans and performance.
  • Avoid vendor catalogues and technical architecture at this stage.
  • Use the diagnostic to let learners discover that integration without master-data governance is incomplete.

Transformation journey

  • Do not present technology as the starting point; begin with process and decision value.
  • Protect the sequence: eliminate, simplify, standardize, automate.
  • Use the simulation to expose high automation with weak data or change management.

Analytics journey

  • Begin every method with a management question.
  • Protect the distinction among the four analytics levels.
  • Use Correlation Lab to demonstrate that statistical relationship is not automatically causation.
